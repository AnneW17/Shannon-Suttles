My honest answer is no, and here is the reasoning so you can overrule me if you disagree. A website is something a person chooses to look at. An email is read in a queue, often one-handed, often in sunlight. Long passages of pale text on black measurably slow reading and tire the eye. Worse, Gmail and Outlook on phones apply their own dark-mode inversion: they see a dark email, invert parts of it, and produce grey text on grey, or a black band across a white page. It is the single most common way a carefully built email arrives looking broken, and it cannot be prevented reliably. So the ink stays where it does its work — the masthead and the footer, wrapping her words top and bottom — and the words themselves sit on the site's own pearl, which is the colour they already sit on when someone reads them on the website. The brand is unmistakable; the writing stays readable.
